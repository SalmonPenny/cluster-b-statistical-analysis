# Cluster B Stage 2E · Candidate selection

**MemOS is the leading candidate for a matched follow-up on HaluMem QA.** The Judge faithfulness gates on Mem0 and LightMem are candidates for selective answering when reduced coverage is acceptable. TRUSTMEM is a narrower candidate for preventing unsupported writes during consolidation. These recommendations concern three distinct outcomes. The corpus supports no general-purpose shortlist for hallucination mitigation across benchmarks.

## Selection rules and evidence inventory

The assessment took all 4,319 verified source cells and 4,370 eligible Stage 2A contrasts as its starting point. The criteria specify source eligibility, outcome priority, local performance and trade-offs, independence requirements for general-purpose claims, and explicit treatment of missing evidence. They were recorded before generating the recommendations. This is a retrospective assessment of known results. The six head-to-head scopes were chosen for outcome relevance, aligned within-panel conditions and interpretable interventions, using the full eligible competitor set in each scope. The 16 shortlist entries include separate gate policies, augmentations, a local disadvantage and an insufficient-evidence decision. They are qualitative decisions with evidence anchors. No cross-benchmark effect or evidence score was calculated. The registry contains 1,138 rows keyed by publication, evaluation block and source configuration ID. It preserves 1,126 upstream configuration IDs. Multiple judges of the same IMLogic open-ended answers remain separate protocol records with the same upstream IDs and a shared dependence group. Design lineage labels group named variants for navigation. RQ2 categories, reported implementation versions, model roles, budgets and interventions are recorded separately. MemOS, MemOS + RootMem, EverMemOS and MemoryOS retain their source labels. The BioMem registry distinguishes HMRU-R, HMRU-I and HMRU-T under the A-Mem lineage, and SIRIN gates remain attached to their specific base system and policy. The benchmark's hallucination category includes fabricated **or incorrect** answers divided by benchmark questions. Omission includes missing, incomplete or partially answered responses. The rubric gives hallucination priority when a response both omits and contradicts or fabricates information (HaluMem Appendix D.2, physical p. 31). MemOS is the sole first-front system in both splits. Its nine joint dominance relationships persist across 0–5 pp tolerances, and each one-objective omission preserves its first front. Compared with Mem0, the hallucination-category reduction is 4.00 pp on Medium and 0.68 pp on Long. The Long difference falls within a 1 pp exploratory tolerance even though joint dominance persists. These pairs have no designated baseline or reported paired uncertainty. The splits share one 20-user reference cohort. HaluMem and the cited MemOS paper share seven authors, so the panel is developer-linked (HaluMem physical pp. 1, 15). Retrieval settings vary by system, including Memobase’s 500-token cap (HaluMem Appendix B.1). Long Memobase is conditional because its reported categories do not close and its retrieval setting is qualified. On the conditional extraction protocol MemOS rejects 44.94% and 28.85% of injected distractors on Medium and Long, the lowest measured false-memory resistance in those panels. Its Long update hallucination rate of 0.29% versus Mem0's 0.03% accompanies update omission of 34.47% versus 98.51%. These operation-specific comparisons use different implementations and cannot establish a matched MemOS advantage. ## Confidence, independent review and reproduction

Confidence is strongest in the documented **direction of a specific within-panel comparison**. Related questions, repeated evaluations, missing uncertainty and comparator restrictions sharply limit generalization. The corpus contains 140 protocol blocks and 28 recorded cohort labels, with zero established aligned independent cross-study replications. IMLogic reuses HaluMem-Medium’s histories (RootMem §3.3, physical p. 3). Its cohort label distinguishes the evaluation protocol and does not establish a separate sample of users. HaluMem-Long also extends the same users. SIRIN reuses LongMemEval questions, and BioMem evaluates a medical subset of RealMem. Recorded cohort labels therefore do not establish disjoint samples. Graph paths between architecture names do not align task definitions, metrics, model versions or judge behavior. Consequently there is no identified common effect for a Bradley–Terry ranking or other pooled architecture estimate.

The independent reviewer audited candidate scope, source metrics and numerical examples against the local publications and checked configuration identity, trade-offs, missingness and selection bias. They independently checked source observation links, reproduced all nine earlier Pareto fronts and the 39 tolerance-robust directed relationships, and verified the Stage 2A contrast inventory. This review verifies the analysis and its qualifications. It provides no replication of the underlying experiments.

All **37 automated tests passed**, including six new tests on endpoint classification, protocol identity, missingness, head-to-head coverage and interval orientation, lower-is-better effects, and shortlist scope. Existing datasets and Stage 2A–2D outputs were preserved. From the repository root with the existing scientific Python environment:
