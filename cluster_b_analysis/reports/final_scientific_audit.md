## Major findings and corrections

1. **HaluMem retrieval settings were misattributed.** The Stage 2E narrative assigned a 500-token cap to MemOS. Appendix B.1 assigns it to Memobase for QA and specifies 250 tokens for updating. The source tables already record the correct assignments. Corrected the narrative and clarified the notebook. Also clarified that omission includes partial/incomplete answers and that the rubric prioritizes hallucination when omissions coexist with fabricated or contradictory content.
2. **Distinct cohort labels can share underlying histories or questions.** RootMem explicitly reuses HaluMem-Medium histories, which HaluMem-Long extends. SIRIN reuses the 500 original LongMemEval questions and filters them to 476 for SimpleMem Table 5. BioMem uses a medical subset of RealMem. These are separate protocol labels with linked data, and the 28 recorded labels cannot be counted as disjoint experimental samples. RMM also uses LongMemEval and MSC. Exact history/item overlap with the other reuses was not established. The notebook and candidate report now identify the confirmed lineage. The raw cohort labels and local analyses are retained because they distinguish evaluated protocols and no cross-lineage estimator was used.

3. **The leading HaluMem QA result is developer-linked.** The author line and affiliations (physical p. 1) and the MemOS developer paper listed as reference [14] (physical p. 15) show seven shared authors, with MemTensor affiliations in the benchmark paper. This creates a concrete risk of favorable workload or configuration choices and strengthens the case for an external evaluation. The overlap supplies no evidence that any measured value is incorrect or that the authors acted improperly. The local comparison remains eligible.

**Minor, scorer attribution.** Stage 2E and the notebook now identify GPT-5.4-mini as SIRIN's Judge gate and Claude Sonnet 5 as the strict-unfaithfulness evaluator. Table 8's verified scope leaves the endpoint of the separate served-accuracy scorer unspecified. The computed values already used those distinctions.

## Candidate decision and selection bias

The registry distinguishes named families, source implementations, protocol-specific rows and variants. MemOS + RootMem does not substitute for MemOS, and HMRU-R, HMRU-I and HMRU-T retain their intervention labels. Protocol-specific records for different judges of the same generated answers preserve evaluators and lineage, and carry no claim of new independent predictions. Named controls and borrowed or unclear baselines are kept distinct through verified IDs and eligibility filters. Quantitative negative results appear alongside focused candidates. Missing matched results are identified separately from unfavorable observed scores.

The shortlist is a retrospective, qualitative decision on this corpus. The 16 configurations or scope decisions are manually anchored in the code and do not amount to an exhaustive selection algorithm or preregistration. Of 29 strict direct-hallucination contrasts, 25 come from the original HaluMem QA table, two from MemGuard and two from TRUSTMEM. All 42 strict-unfaithfulness contrasts come from SIRIN Table 8. HaluMem, SIRIN and original LongMemEval receive prominent coverage because their panels provide the direct rubric, selective-answering trade-off and controlled diagnostic set needed by RQ3. This choice does not calibrate those benchmarks to one another. The field's incomplete cross-study coverage makes selection partly evidence-availability driven. A candidate with no eligible direct comparison may still be promising and needs a protocol-aligned test. Stage 2E retains the wider matrix so the shortlist can be inspected against alternative outcomes and interventions.

MemOS remains the strongest local HaluMem QA candidate. SIRIN remains a selective-answering intervention for prospective evaluation, with clear coverage losses and subgroup dependence. TRUSTMEM remains a consolidation hypothesis in one reported workload, with no matched downstream validation. The main findings in these scopes survive the audit. Evidence does not justify a general-purpose candidate tier or architecture-family superiority across benchmarks.
