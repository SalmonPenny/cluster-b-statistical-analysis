# Statistical feasibility after the Stage 1 continuation

The estimand should be explicit for each result. A complete-system contrast asks how two evaluated pipelines perform under one protocol. A component contrast asks what changes when the paper varies a defined memory mechanism while holding the documented backbone, task, scorer and other settings fixed. The present data can support some of each. An RQ2 category-level effect or a universal anti-hallucination ranking is currently unidentified.

## Supported descriptive analyses

| Method | Formulation and interpretation | Requirements and present use |
|---|---|---|
| Within-block scores and ranks | Order scores after orienting the metric. Use midranks for exact ties and keep score differences alongside ranks. An average rank is an explicit average over complete, commensurable blocks. | Appropriate descriptively within documented blocks. Reporting a rank across different baseline sets rewards and penalizes systems according to who else was included. An aggregated rank requires a target task distribution, comparable eligibility and a principled missingness treatment. |
| Matched baseline differences | For a benefit measure on a proportion scale, report Δ = p_method − p_baseline, with percentage points as the presentation unit. For a genuine error complement, relative error reduction is Δ/(1 − p_baseline) when the denominator is positive. For a directly labeled bad-event rate, use (e_baseline − e_method)/e_baseline when defined. | Appropriate for verified matched protocols. State baseline, absolute scores, task and scorers. Error reduction in judge incorrectness has a different meaning from reduction in hallucination. Avoid computing either formulation for metric scales without the required semantics. |
| Pareto analysis | Within a protocol, A dominates B when A is at least as good on every included objective and strictly better on one, after orienting directions. | Descriptively possible for a joint accuracy/resource or correctness/hallucination/omission panel with matched, independently eligible rows. Token units and aggregation scope must agree. A Pareto front based on printed means has unknown sampling stability. |
| Normalized composites | A weighted score ∑ w_j f_j(x_j) requires explicit orientations, scale transforms f_j, weights summing to one and aligned meaning. Percentage-to-proportion is merely a unit conversion. | Defer cross-benchmark composites. Baseline standardization changes the target when baselines differ and min–max scaling changes with the candidate set. If criteria become defensible, vary normalization and weights and publish the raw outcome vector. |

In RMM Table 1, the published GTE QA figures yield a matched descriptive difference of 6.8 percentage points (70.4% versus 63.6%) across the two evaluated pipelines. The published averages over three runs come without dispersion, so the difference has no recoverable interval from that table. For paired binary item-level observations later, the variance of the difference depends on discordant outcomes. Marginal scores and denominators alone leave the covariance unidentified. Shared items, histories, benchmark seeds and judges additionally induce dependence. Where raw data are obtained, preserve paired predictions, resample by independently sampled history or conversation with model outputs kept together, and describe how runs and judges enter the estimand. Ten conversations warrant particular caution about resampling precision. Keep reported run counts separate from item counts.

## What the added evidence supports

| Analysis | Stage 1 decision |
|---|---|
| Within-benchmark rankings | Rank systems descriptively within a single benchmark release, metric and protocol. HaluMem v3 QA shares scorer and generator within each split. Agent-Native Figure 9 permits per-backbone LoCoMo ranks on its own reported outcomes. Separate the four Fig. 9 backbones because they reuse the workload. |
| Matched baseline-relative performance | Compare paired source contrasts such as MemGuard versus its rerun A-Mem on documented subcategories or LongMemEval’s matched reader and retrieval design cells. SIRIN reports paired cluster-bootstrap gains for Mem0 and LightMem. The gains describe selective served accuracy under retrospective thresholds. |
| Pareto dominance | Compare accuracy, strict unfaithfulness, coverage and token use within a compatible SIRIN panel, with all objectives and denominators stated. Mem0 with both judge gates scores 51.0% served accuracy with 38.0% abstention. Its faithfulness-only judge gate scores 42.8% with 20.0% abstention and 23.2% strict unfaithfulness, against 28.7% for both gates. Neither dominates on these three objectives. Generator-only token change excludes the detector. |
| Failure modes | Report descriptive within-panel comparisons for temporal evidence retrieval, knowledge updating, cross-session reasoning, false-premise rejection, direct write/QA hallucination labels and transition errors. Report matched adverse outcomes beside accuracy and keep the LoCoMo and HaluMem proxies distinct. |
| Sensitivity | Study and benchmark deletion, strict versus relaxed inclusion, architecture grouping and endpoint-weight sensitivity are feasible on supported subsets. The graph deletion results are already saved. Weight changes require a complete jointly comparable outcome set and sensitivity to coverage and cohort definitions. |
| Regularized and Bayesian Bradley–Terry | Defer data-driven probabilistic rankings in the current components. Even the strict label projection is assembled from unmatched protocols. A within-source, single-cohort ordering supplies little independent information about transfer and ranking uncertainty. Reconsider a narrowly specified scope when paired items or replicated compatible comparison blocks are available. |

SIRIN Table 8 is the clearest new uncertainty example. The paired judge-and-both-gates gain is +13.2 percentage points (95% interval +10.1 to +16.4) on Mem0 and +14.3 (+11.0 to +17.7) on LightMem. Both intervals come from the authors’ dependency-cluster bootstrap and apply to their within-system policy contrasts. Served QA gains are conditional on answering, and the experimental coverage cut is chosen on the evaluated cohort. The two effects share a benchmark workload and cannot be treated as independent replications of a memory architecture effect. The estimates, original units and intervals are in the validated database.

The evidence ledger has 28 strict headline block IDs grouped into 15 conservative cohort groups. There are 18 cohort groups even when direct component and diagnostic comparisons are included. Counts of system pairs, outcomes, categories and seeds are larger because many use the same items. Across studies the same released item set can create additional dependence. A cross-protocol likelihood would need an estimand, overlapping architecture/backbone/scorer configurations, and comparative data with covariance information. Within narrow cohorts, complete rankings and repeated wins generated from the same printed outcome do not create independent Bernoulli outcomes. A reference constraint sets the location of a Bradley–Terry model; regularization or a prior can stabilize parameters but cannot identify cross-protocol effects or quantify data-supported transfer into unobserved comparisons. ## Models and tests to reassess with further extraction

| Method | Formulation and interpretation | Evidence requirement and decision |
|---|---|---|
| Regularized Bradley–Terry | Model P(i beats j) = logistic(θ_i − θ_j). A sum-to-zero or reference constraint sets the location. A ridge penalty stabilizes estimates under separation. Specify a tie rule or a tie-aware model. | Requires defensibly exchangeable comparisons and informative wins/losses under a common outcome. Connectedness of the undirected evidence graph is necessary within each scope, and unpenalized finite estimates further require directed support. A ridge prior cannot supply missing empirical contrasts. Do not fit globally now. |
| Bayesian Bradley–Terry | Use the same likelihood with centered, weakly informative priors and report posterior probabilities of pairwise superiority and sensitivity to scale. Define a practical-equivalence region on the comparison outcome or use an effect-size-based tie definition. | Revisit within a compatible, adequately replicated component. Avoid expanding a single multi-system rank into independent Bernoulli wins or stacking related metrics as wins. A ranking likelihood per independent block or a clustered/hierarchical comparison structure would be required. Priors can dominate weak links or separated designs. |
| Failure-mode-specific probabilistic ranking | Within comparable mode strata, estimate θ_(architecture, mode), with partial pooling only when modes and measurement links support it. Report superiority for stated modes and practical margins. | Requires replicated, calibrated measurements within a failure mode and a connected contrast design for it. Mixed LoCoMo/LongMemEval temporal questions and HaluMem operation labels do not give calibrated outcomes. Stage 1 supports descriptive profiles and coverage only. |
| Friedman and Nemenyi | Friedman tests mean within-block rank differences over independent complete blocks. Nemenyi contrasts mean ranks with a multiple-comparison critical difference after an appropriate omnibus analysis. | Few independent complete blocks with the same systems, and overlapping data/metrics, make these unsuitable now. Subsets or repeated scores cannot stand in for independent benchmark blocks. |
| Skillings–Mack | A rank test for incomplete blocks. It centers ranks within blocks and accounts for which treatments co-occur via the rank-sum covariance. | Requires a connected, informative design, genuinely independent blocks and a defensible absence mechanism. Method availability and selective reporting can be outcome-related. No present test is justified. |
| Network meta-analysis | Model within-study contrasts y_s,ij around d_i − d_j, using reported within-study covariance and, if supported, between-study heterogeneity. Explore direct–indirect agreement in loops. | Requires a common outcome, adequately matched intervention definitions, estimating variances and credible transitivity across effect modifiers. Baseline name overlap and connectivity do not satisfy these requirements. |
| Hierarchical Bayesian regression | With raw paired binary outcomes or valid effect estimates, include architecture, protocol and task terms, shared item/study effects and, if supported, architecture-by-mode interactions. | Requires variation separating architecture from backbone, judge and retrieval setup, replication at each modeled level, uncertainty and model checking. With aggregate means and little comparable overlap, unidentified terms would be prior-driven. |

Sensitivity analyses should first track evidence inclusion. Compare strict versus relaxed scope, remove one study at a time and one benchmark at a time, and report which within-scope contrasts remain identified. Separate consensus across metrics from sensitivity to metric weights. If later fitting is justified, vary priors, method/version grouping, treatment of ties, benchmark weighting, normalization and imported-result exclusions. Report identified changes in magnitude and uncertainty, plus connectivity after each removal. Respect the nested structure of QA categories and avoid counting Overall alongside its constituents as an additional study.

## Sources consulted selectively

- Wainer’s abstract describes modeling dataset-level wins and practical equivalence, including a local region of practical equivalence. [Wainer (2023)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v24/22-0907.html)
- Demšar’s abstract presents the independent-dataset comparison setting and rank-based procedures for multiple algorithms. [Demšar (2006)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v7/demsar06a.html)
- Benavoli et al.’s abstract motivates Bayesian alternatives to null hypothesis testing. [Benavoli et al. (2017)](https://www.jmlr.org/papers/v18/16-305.html)
- Agarwal et al.’s project page links tools for interval estimates and aggregate performance under limited runs. These ideas require access to the appropriate paired units here. [Agarwal et al. (2021)](https://agarwl.github.io/rliable/)
- HELM’s abstract motivates explicit scenario-by-metric coverage and comparisons under standardized conditions. [Liang et al. (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2211.09110)
